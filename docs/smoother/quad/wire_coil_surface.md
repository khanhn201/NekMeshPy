# Helical wire surface

The wire's surface in the `examples/wire_coil.py` hex mesh (face tag `coil`), smoothed as a free-standing surface with
its open rim frozen, as built or with perturbed input.

```{include} _generated/wire_coil_surface.viewer.inc
```

## Key points

- **The target surface is circular**: the wire has no CAD description, so its vertices are held to a tessellation of
  the mesh's own curved quads. This shows how the smoother redistributes vertices on a given surface, not a check
  against an independent geometry.
- **Vertices move a lot**: the helical template skews its quads by design, so vertices move by whole local edges (the
  lines below the table). As built, the minimum barely changes while the mean and the worst 5 % improve.
- **The smoother does not touch the hexes**, and moving the surface this far on its own does not leave valid layers
  around it ({ref}`wire-coil-hexes`). The surface numbers are not volume-mesh quality.
- *Perturbed input*: every movable vertex shifted by up to 0.2 of its local edge length, then put back on the surface.

## The hexes

- `examples/smoother/quad/cs_wire_coil_hex.py` takes the order-1 hex mesh (the corner geometry a `.re2` file holds) and puts
  the smoothed surface's vertices in place of the old ones; every other vertex stays where the example put it.
- Measured at the 8 corners (what `.re2` holds) and re-read on a finer lattice at order 7, the solver's.

<details>
<summary>Notes and caveats</summary>

- `coil` separates fluid from wire, so `hexmesh.boundary_mesh(mesh, "coil")` returns every quad twice, once per side;
  the script keeps one of each pair.
- The surface is too large for the data to keep a stage history, so the viewer shows only the input and the final state
  (panels and histogram as in {doc}`simple_geometries`).
- The tables are regenerated by `docs/_ext/gen_smoother_quad_assets.py` and `docs/_ext/gen_smoother_quad_hexviews_assets.py`; nothing
  is typed in.
